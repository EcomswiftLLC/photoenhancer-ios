# Cold Resolve

A visual philosophy for the Clarivo mark.

Form exists in two states at once: the coarse and the resolved. A single surface is cut cleanly in two, one side built
from a few large, quiet blocks of colour, the other continuous, smooth and exact. The cut itself is the subject: a thin
line of cold light where approximation becomes certainty. Nothing else is needed, and nothing else is allowed.

Colour is taken from deep water and winter air: a blue-black ink that is never neutral, a mineral teal, and one glacial
aqua reserved for the moment of resolution. Warm tones are absent. Light never blooms; it is clear, edged and directional,
the way light reads through ice. Every value is meticulously calibrated so the blocks and the smooth field are the same
scene, just at different resolutions, and the eye can travel across the line without a jump in hue.

Space is generous and centred. The tile floats in the ink with a wide margin so that the mark survives at the size of a
fingernail and still reads as one gesture: blocks, line, clarity. Scale is used with restraint; the blocks are large
enough to be counted, the smooth half is calm enough to rest on.

A single corner mark, two strokes meeting at a right angle, sits at the resolved edge like the registration corner of a
precision instrument. It is the only ornament, and it earns its place by pointing at the clear side.

Execution must look like the product of painstaking, master-level craft: supersampled edges, gaps between blocks
measured to the pixel, gradients without banding, a line whose glow is felt rather than seen. The work is finished when
removing any element would break it and adding any element would cheapen it.
